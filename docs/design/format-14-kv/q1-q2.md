# Format 14's two owner questions, measured

Targeted measurements for the owner's Q1 (split or inline, `format-14-kv.md` D1b) and Q2 (the
codec per tier, D1d). Not a gate run: in-RAM micro-prototypes, minutes long, run-level
interleaved with an A/A, on the development Mac and the Linux gate VM.

## Plan (written before the runs)

### Q1. Split against inline, with a seek that starts at its target

**Hypothesis.** Split's 3× seek gap is termdict's `reader::scan`, which opens the floor block and
builds every key from the block's start (up to 63 of them) before reaching `from`. A seek that
binary-searches the block's restarts and positions inside the run without building keys (the
`find_from` matching rule, then the found key rebuilt as `from`'s shared prefix plus its suffix)
closes most of it, leaving split within 1.3× of inline on seek and 10. Gets stay within 15%.

**Procedure.** `bench/kv/proto/cached.cc` (`kv_proto_cached`), 1,000,000 records at 100 B and at
1 KiB, 4 KiB blocks, LZ4. Every operation goes through D2's CLOCK cache (the cache prototype's
structure, `clock_cache.hh`) holding decoded and parsed blocks, its capacity calibrated per run so
that YCSB's scrambled-zipfian gets reach a block hit rate of 60%, 90%, and 99% (RocksDB's audit saw
99.3% at fits, 93% on YCSB C, 39-61% at 2×, `rocksdb-mechanisms.md` 2.7). A miss decodes into a
recycled buffer. Operations, each drawn from scrambled zipfian:

- get of a present key, get of an absent key (no filter in either layout: the fuse filter sits in
  front of both and answers 99.6% of absent gets, so this row prices only its false positives),
- seek and 10, YCSB E's scan (length uniform in 1 to 100),
- a full scan from the compressed bytes (no cache), and the build per record.

Split seeks with the new cursor (`td_cursor`, written against termdict's public block API, the
shape `termdict::reader::cursor::seek` would take). Bytes: data, index, and key dictionary per
logical byte.

**Keep or remove.** Split stays recommended if, with the seek, its seek and 10 and YCSB E are within
1.3× of inline's and its gets within 15%, on both platforms. If seek and 10 stays past 1.5× on
either, inline is the better shape for the key-value core and the recommendation flips.

### Q2. LZ4 everywhere, zstd 1 on merged output, zstd 1 everywhere

**Hypothesis.** zstd 1's decode costs about 3 µs more per 4 KiB block than LZ4's, so the get
latency gap is about (1 - hit) × 3 µs: about 1.2 µs (over 50%) at 60%, 0.3 µs at 90%, 0.03 µs
(noise) at 99%. Disk: zstd 1 about 16% smaller than LZ4.

**Procedure.** The same program on the split layout at each of the three hit rates, with codecs
`lz4`, `zstd1`, and `mixed` (zstd 1 on merged output, LZ4 on flushes: 10% of blocks LZ4, chosen
by a hash of the block number, the share of data in unmerged flushed segments, an assumption
stated as such). `--mode merge` times a 4-way merge of key-ordered sources into one output (keys
merged through termdict cursors, values read sequentially by decoding each source's blocks, the
output's termdict and value blocks written) for lz4 to lz4, lz4 to zstd 1, and zstd 1 to zstd 1.
Build and merge CPU are per record.

**Keep or remove.** zstd 1 on merged output is the recommended preset if its get latency is within
10% of LZ4's at 90% hits and its disk at least 10% below LZ4's; the hit rate where the gap falls
under 5% is reported as the break-even.

### Discipline

Each round runs every configuration once, alternating direction per round; split LZ4 runs twice per
round (A/A) for the noise floor. Three rounds per platform, single-threaded, under light holds.

## Results

Run on 2026-10-20 at `785bf19`. **Mac**: i9-9980HK, macOS 15.8.1, Apple clang 17, LZ4 1.10.0.
**VM**: the Linux gate VM (Ubuntu 24.04, QEMU/HVF on the same host, 8 vCPUs, 16 GB), clang 18.1.3,
LZ4 1.9.4. zstd 1.5.7 (pinned) on both. Release builds, one thread, light holds, three rounds
interleaved with the direction alternating; medians quoted. Raw lines: `q12-mac.jsonl.gz`,
`q12-vm.jsonl.gz`; every median at the end of this file (`run.py report`).

**Noise.** The A/A pair (split LZ4 twice per round) differs by at most 3.5% on gets, 4.0% on seek
and 10, 3.8% on YCSB E (median 1.1% to 2.1%), and 7.7% on absent gets; run-to-run spreads are
mostly under 10%. Differences under about 5% below are noise. Achieved block hit rates on gets:
59.8-60.8%, 89.9-90.5%, 99.0-99.2% for the three targets, the same for every layout and codec.

**Correctness.** Every split run first checks 4,096 seeks of present keys (each lands on itself
with its own ordinal) and 4,096 of absent keys (each lands on `lower_bound`'s key), for both seek
modes; every merge checks it emitted every record once. The prototypes ran clean under ASan and
UBSan at 50,000 records in every mode.

**Limits.** In RAM: one segment, no mmap faults, no region checksums, no filter, no docids or live
bits; the termdict and inline's first-key index are read in place and never miss, while value
blocks go through the cache. The numbers choose between options; the gate rows measure the engine.

### Q1. Split against inline (LZ4, 4 KiB blocks, 1,000,000 records)

Ratios are split's time against inline's. "Seek" is split with the target-starting seek
(`td_cursor`); "walk" is split positioning as termdict's `reader::scan` does today.

| Measure | 100 B, Mac | 100 B, VM | 1 KiB, Mac | 1 KiB, VM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Get, present key, 60 / 90 / 99% hits | 1.21× / 1.11× / 1.00× slower | 1.02× slower / 1.14× faster / 1.06× faster | 1.11× / 1.13× / 1.26× slower | 1.05× slower / 1.04× faster / 1.02× slower |
| Get, absent key (no filter), all hit rates | 1.47-2.12× faster | 1.97-2.33× faster | 2.00-2.53× faster | 2.42-2.77× faster |
| Seek and 10, seek, 60 / 90 / 99% | 1.40× / 1.44× / 1.42× slower | 1.11× / 1.16× / 1.09× slower | 1.19× / 1.25× / 1.32× slower | 1.04× / 1.02× / 1.02× slower |
| Seek and 10, walk (today) | 2.03× / 2.18× / 2.14× slower | 1.49× / 1.49× / 1.52× slower | 1.34× / 1.55× / 1.66× slower | 1.17× / 1.23× / 1.24× slower |
| YCSB E scan (1-100 records), seek | 1.62× / 1.82× / 1.88× slower | 1.31× / 1.31× / 1.43× slower | 1.21× / 1.20× / 1.34× slower | 1.00× / 1.02× / 1.06× slower |
| Full scan, ns per record (no cache) | 48.5 vs 25.6: 1.89× slower | 51.4 vs 23.4: 2.20× slower | 155.6 vs 125.5: 1.24× slower | 165.1 vs 130.8: 1.26× slower |
| of which termdict's key walk alone | 32.2 ns per key | 32.5 ns per key | 36.0 ns per key | 32.9 ns per key |
| Bytes per logical byte | 0.570 vs 0.591: 3.6% smaller | same | 0.568 vs 0.578: 1.7% smaller | same |

| Cache for the same hit rate (99%) | 136 vs 149 MiB: 9% less | same | 1,791 MiB each | same |

**What the seek closed.** Starting at the target instead of the block's first key makes split's
seek and 10 1.45× faster on the Mac and 1.34× on the VM at 100 B (2,826 to 1,944 ns; 3,166 to
2,358 ns at 60% hits), taking the gap to inline from 2.0-2.2× to 1.4× on the Mac and from 1.5× to
1.1× on the VM. Gets, absent gets, and full scans do not use it and do not move (within the A/A).

**What remains, and where.** Short scans at 100 B: each record of a scan costs split one termdict
entry decode, measured alone at 32-36 ns per key on both platforms, more than inline's whole record
(23-26 ns per record in its full scan, LZ4 decode included); split's value side adds only 16-19 ns
(its full scan minus the key walk).
That is termdict's sequential decode (the alphabet-coded suffix unpacked symbol by symbol, the
header's LEB128s, the ordinal), not the layout: at 1 KiB, where values dominate, split and inline
are within 6% on every read on Linux. One cheap fix was tried and does not carry it: hoisting the
division in `suffixes::decode` (`57 / width_` per call) moved the key walk from 35.1 to 32.0 ns
and YCSB E by 5%, inside the noise of three trials, so it is not the cost.

**What inline duplicates.** On disk, nothing: inline replaces `keys`' termdict. Its key encoding is
larger, though: about 12.5 B per record of prefix-coded keys inside the blocks (67.8 against
55.3 B of block data) plus a 0.78 B first-key index, 13.3 B against split's 10.9 B (the termdict's
10.46 B and the value-block index's 0.40 B), 22% more. In code it is a second sorted-key structure
beside termdict, which the search layout keeps: a builder, a bounds-checked block reader, its seek
and cursor, a block index, its own `check()`, fuzz target, and filter wiring. The prototype's is
137 lines of layout and 79 of cursor; termdict, the production analog, is 547 lines plus 254 of
tests and a fuzz target, with fst's 677 behind its index. Split adds `cursor::seek`, about 70 lines
in termdict.

**Against the plan's criteria.** Neither fired cleanly: seek and 10 met the 1.3× bar on the VM
(1.09-1.16×) but not on the Mac (1.40-1.44×), YCSB E missed it at 100 B on both (1.31-1.43× VM,
1.62-1.88× Mac), and nothing passed the 1.5× flip bar on seek and 10. Gets met the 15% bar
everywhere but the Mac's 1 KiB at 99% (1.26×).

### Q2. LZ4 everywhere, zstd 1 on merged output, zstd 1 everywhere (split, 4 KiB)

"Merged" is `mixed`: 90% of blocks zstd 1, 10% LZ4. Percentages are against LZ4 at the same hit
rate; the A/A is ±3%.

| Measure | 100 B, Mac | 100 B, VM | 1 KiB, Mac | 1 KiB, VM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disk per logical byte: LZ4 / merged / zstd 1 | 0.570 / 0.489 / 0.480 | same | 0.568 / 0.480 / 0.470 | same |
| Merged, zstd 1 against LZ4 on disk | 14% / 16% smaller | same | 15% / 17% smaller | same |
| Get at 60% hits: merged / zstd 1 | +92% / +100% | +65% / +75% | +57% / +64% | +40% / +45% |
| Get at 90% hits | +28% / +32% | +26% / +25% | +15% / +21% | +16% / +12% |
| Get at 99% hits | +4% / +2% | -4% / -4% | +1% / +1% | +3% / +3% |
| Seek and 10 at 60 / 90 / 99%, merged | +75% / +22% / -1% | +60% / +15% / +1% | +89% / +30% / +5% | +72% / +27% / +7% |
| YCSB E at 60 / 90 / 99%, merged | +69% / +14% / +4% | +64% / +22% / -1% | +110% / +38% / +2% | +97% / +35% / +3% |
| Full scan per record (no cache): LZ4 / merged / zstd 1 | 48.5 / 110 / 118 ns | 51.4 / 113 / 124 ns | 156 / 840 / 914 ns | 165 / 860 / 926 ns |
| Flush build per record: LZ4 / zstd 1 (merged flushes LZ4) | 365 / 514 ns: 1.41× | 325 / 508 ns: 1.56× | 2,032 / 3,786 ns: 1.86× | 1,762 / 3,918 ns: 2.22× |
| 4-way merge per record: LZ4→LZ4 / LZ4→zstd 1 / zstd 1→zstd 1 | 431 / 581 / 660 ns | 369 / 564 / 633 ns | 2,072 / 3,960 / 4,695 ns | 1,846 / 4,046 / 4,912 ns |
| zstd 1's extra decode per missed block | 3.2-3.3 µs | 3.2 µs | 2.5-2.7 µs | 2.0-2.3 µs |
| Hit rate where a get's gap falls under 5% | 98.6% | 98.0% | 97.4% | 96.1% |

The gap follows (1 - hit rate) times the per-miss decode delta, as planned: 3.2 µs at 100 B from
both the 60% and the 90% rows. **zstd's extra decode stops mattering at about 98% block hits on
gets (96-99% across sizes and platforms; YCSB E needs 97.7-98.6%)**, that is, only when the
working set fits the cache. RocksDB's audit saw 39-61% at the 2× sets and 93% on YCSB C
(`rocksdb-mechanisms.md` 2.7), where zstd adds 65-100% and about 26% to a get. Merged and zstd 1
everywhere read alike (90% of blocks are zstd either way); merged flushes at LZ4's cost.

**In dollars, at stated prices** (block storage $0.08 per GB-month, $0.04 per core-hour, both
assumptions): zstd 1 on merged output saves 81-88 GB per logical TB stored, about $6.5-7 a month.
It costs about 0.32 µs of CPU per get at 90% hits and 1.3 µs at 60%, which equals the disk saving
at about 680,000 gets per second per logical TB sustained at 90% hits, or 170,000 at 60%. Its merge
CPU, 0.2 µs per 100 B record per merge pass (about 0.5 core-hours per logical TB per pass at
100 B), is negligible. So on cost alone zstd on merged output wins for any index read below those
rates, which is most of the many-index workload's bytes; on latency, which the gate rows measure
against RocksDB's LZ4, it loses below 98% hits.

## Decision tables

### Q1. The value layout

| Option | Measured (VM, the gate platform; Mac in parentheses) | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Split** with termdict `cursor::seek` | Gets 1.14× faster to 1.02× slower (Mac 1.00-1.26× slower); absent gets 2.0-2.8× faster (Mac 1.5-2.5×); seek and 10 1.09-1.16× slower at 100 B, 1.02-1.04× at 1 KiB (Mac 1.19-1.44×); YCSB E 1.31-1.43× slower at 100 B, 1.00-1.06× at 1 KiB (Mac 1.20-1.88×); full scan 2.2× slower at 100 B, 1.26× at 1 KiB; 1.7-3.6% less disk; build 1.10-1.28× slower; one key structure | **Recommended**, with two conditions: termdict gains `cursor::seek` (step 1, additive), and a termdict speed pass on its sequential decode (32-36 ns per key today) before step 2's scan gate (T5). It is the whole remaining gap, at 100 B scans only, and prospect's term iteration gains from it too |
| Inline (RocksDB's block table) | The fastest short scans at 100 B (1.3-1.9× faster than split) and full scans (2.2× at 100 B); gets at parity on Linux; absent gets 2-2.8× slower without the filter (only its 0.39% false positives reach them in practice); 22% larger key encoding and 9% more cache for the same hit rate at 100 B; a second key structure with its own reader, seek, `check()`, and fuzz target | The fallback if termdict's decode cannot reach about 15 ns per key; then the 100 B short-scan gap is the layout's, and inline pays for its code |

### Q2. The codec per tier

| Option | Measured (Mac / VM) | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **LZ4 everywhere** | Fastest reads at every hit rate; fastest flush (325-365 ns per 100 B record) and merge (369-431 ns); disk 0.570 (100 B) and 0.568 (1 KiB) per logical byte | **Recommended default**: the project's fastest-measured-preset rule, and T4's 2× rows run where zstd costs most (39-61% hits). It concedes T7's second clause (within 10% of format 13's disk, about 31% above by the design's estimate) unless rank ordinals and a later raw-copy merge recover it; that clause is the owner's to relax or keep |
| zstd 1 on merged output, LZ4 on flushes | 14-15.5% less disk than LZ4; gets +57-92% / +40-65% at 60% hits, +15-28% / +16-26% at 90%, within noise at 99%; YCSB E +64-110% at 60%, +14-38% at 90%; full scans 2.2-5.4× slower; merge 1.35-1.9× / 1.53-2.2× LZ4's CPU, flush as LZ4 | **The disk preset** (the codec byte makes it a writer setting, no format change), for disk-bound hosts and cold data: cheaper in dollars below about 10^5-10^6 gets per second per logical TB, free in latency at 98% hits and above. Not the default |
| zstd 1 everywhere | Reads as merged (within noise); 1.5-2% less disk than merged; flush 1.4-2.2× LZ4's CPU | Not recommended: merged gives the same disk within 2% with LZ4-cost flushes |
